# MovieFlo.AI Filmmaking Workflow and Process Framework - ChatGPT-5

## Project Initiation – Signup and New Project Setup

 **Creating an Account and Starting a Project:** To use MovieFlo.AI, users first **sign up for an account** (using a Google login or email/password) [oai_citation:0‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=First%20thing%20you%20will%20need,all%20there%20is%20to%20it). Once logged in, you can initiate a new project from the **Explore** dashboard by clicking “**Create New Project**” (also labeled **“Concept to Video”** in the app) [oai_citation:1‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=1,or%20Concept%20to%20Video%20button). This opens a guided project setup interface (shown above) where you configure the initial project settings. Key steps and options in project initiation include:

1. **Choose a Concept Source:** Decide how to kick off your project. You can either **“Develop concept with AI”** using MovieFlo’s AI Co-Pilot (for an interactive brainstorming workflow), or **upload your own script/brief** if you have pre-written material [oai_citation:2‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=1,or%20Concept%20to%20Video%20button). MovieFlo supports uploading PDF or text script files (note: extremely long scripts may have limitations) [oai_citation:3‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=2,scripts%20may%20currently%20have%20issues).

2. **Select a Story Type:** Pick the general type of project. **Short Film** is the default for narrative stories, **Commercial/Ad** is tailored for advertisement projects (with extra ad-specific fields), or **Custom** for an open format [oai_citation:4‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=3,to%20add%20Ad%20Campaign%20Details). Choosing **Commercial/Ad** will prompt additional **ad campaign details** to be filled in (such as product name, target audience, key message, and call-to-action) to guide the ad creation [oai_citation:5‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=3,to%20add%20Ad%20Campaign%20Details) [oai_citation:6‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/marketers-agencies#:~:text=1). (For example, the Ad Builder interface lets you drop in your product info, define the audience, and set the campaign’s tone easily [oai_citation:7‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/marketers-agencies#:~:text=1).) If Short Film or Custom is chosen, the workflow focuses on narrative structure without the marketing-specific prompts.

3. **Output Target (Platform):** Specify where the final video will be used – e.g. **YouTube, Instagram, TikTok,** etc. Selecting an output target presets the appropriate **aspect ratio and duration** for that platform (for instance, TikTok might auto-set a 9:16 vertical frame and a shorter length) [oai_citation:8‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=4.%20Output%20Target%20,YouTube%2C%20instagram%2C%20Tiktok%20and%20more). You can leave this as “General” or choose a specific platform to have MovieFlo adjust technical settings accordingly.

4. **Format – Aspect Ratio & Duration:** Further refine the format by explicitly setting the **aspect ratio** and desired **duration** of the video. The interface offers common aspect ratios (**16:9**, **3:2** widescreen, **9:16**, **2:3** vertical, or **1:1** square) and a target length (e.g. 30s, 60s) [oai_citation:9‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Available%20choices%20include%20YouTube%2C%20instagram%2C,Tiktok%20and%20more) [oai_citation:10‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=5,adjusted%20during%20the%20creation%20process). These can be adjusted later, but they inform the initial project canvas (changing aspect ratio later will require regenerating visuals to the new format [oai_citation:11‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=have%20to%20regenerate%20any%20image,for%20this%20to%20take%20effect)).

5. **Default AI Models:** Optionally choose default generation models to use for this project. You can pick a default **video model**, **image model**, and **lip-sync model** here (for instance, set video generation to *Veo 3 (Quality)* for high fidelity, image generation to *Kontext Pro*, and lipsync to *Tavus (Hummingbird*) by default) [oai_citation:12‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=6.%20Default%20Models%20,shot%20via%20a%20pulldown%20menu). These defaults apply globally but can be overridden per shot during creation. (They can also be changed later in Project Settings if needed.)

6. **Story Structure:** Decide the narrative structure. Options include **AI-Determined** (let the AI decide an appropriate structure based on the story type), **Acts** (e.g. a three-act structure for a film), or **Episodic** (for multi-episode or segmented content) [oai_citation:13‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=individual%20shot%20via%20a%20pulldown,menu). For ads, the structure might be a single sequence, whereas a short film might use acts. AI-Determined will automatically choose a sensible structure (e.g. for a 30-second ad it might not use acts, for a short film it might use a three-act breakdown).

7. **Genre and Tone:** Set the **genre** (e.g. drama, comedy, sci-fi, fantasy, etc.) and **tone** of your story. Genre defines the style or category of the story, and tone influences the mood (uplifting, suspenseful, humorous, etc.) [oai_citation:14‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=8.%20Genre%20and%20Tone%20,emotional%20impact%20of%20your%20script). You can select from presets or type in custom tags. These choices guide the AI in shaping the script and visuals to fit the desired mood and style.

8. **Visual Style:** Select an overall visual/art style for the project. MovieFlo offers a wide range of stylistic presets – for example, *Photorealistic* styles (like “16mm indie film”, “1970’s film” look, “Cyberpunk neon-noir”, “Documentary”, “Golden Hour” lighting), or *Animated* aesthetics (such as **anime** styles including shōnen or moe anime, **claymation**, **puppet**/stop-motion, **modern comic book**, **Voxel art**, etc.) [oai_citation:15‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,of%20styles%20can%20I%20create) [oai_citation:16‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=9.%20Style%20,for%20each%20Story%20you%20create). You can experiment with these; the chosen style will affect both storyboard imagery and final video to ensure consistency. (The style can be changed later via Settings, though you’d need to regenerate images/videos with the new style to see the effect [oai_citation:17‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,for%20this%20to%20take%20effect) [oai_citation:18‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,before%20you%20made%20the%20change).)

9. **Confirm and Create:** After filling in the above, you proceed to create the project. The project is now initialized with your settings. At this point, if you chose **Develop with AI**, the MovieFlo **Co-Pilot (“Flo”)** will guide you through concept development. If you uploaded a script, the system will parse it and jump ahead to scene and actor generation using your script as input [oai_citation:19‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=1,or%20Concept%20to%20Video%20button).

**Pro Tip:** All these project settings (story type, platform, aspect ratio, style, etc.) are not permanently locked – you can adjust them later in the **Settings** cog during the project, and the content will update accordingly (e.g. changing Tone or Genre will influence regenerated scripts; changing Art Style or Aspect Ratio will require regenerating images to apply the new style/format) [oai_citation:20‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=have%20to%20regenerate%20any%20image,for%20this%20to%20take%20effect) [oai_citation:21‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,before%20you%20made%20the%20change). This gives flexibility to refine the creative direction even after the project has started.

## Concept Development – Brief, Synopsis, and Scene Breakdown

Once the project is created, you enter the **Concept** stage. This is where you flesh out your idea with the help of MovieFlo’s AI assistant (Flo). The interface is divided into steps or tabs (Concept → Synopsis → Scenes → Script, etc.), and Flo will walk you through developing a narrative foundation from scratch (or expanding your provided brief). The concept development process involves several sub-steps:

- **Describe Your Idea (Concept Tab):** In the **Concept** tab, you start by writing a **brief description of your story or ad idea** in natural language [oai_citation:22‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,or%20complex%20as%20you%20like). For example, you might describe the premise, the main character’s goal, the product you’re advertising (if an ad), or any key elements you envision. This can be a few sentences or a longer paragraph – *it’s essentially your initial creative brief*. As you write, the AI Co-Pilot (Flo) may prompt you with follow-up questions or suggestions. Flo engages in a short Q&A to refine the concept: it might ask about the setting, the protagonist’s traits, target audience (for ads), key product features to highlight, desired emotional tone, etc., depending on your project [oai_citation:23‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Flo%E2%80%99s%20suggestions%20at%20every%20step). You can answer these questions or pick from Flo’s suggestions, mix your ideas with AI suggestions, or skip some – it’s an interactive brainstorming. The goal is to gather enough detail for the AI to draft the story outline.

- **Generate a Synopsis:** After refining the concept and answering Flo’s prompts, you click **“Generate Synopsis.”** The AI then produces a brief **synopsis** – a short summary of the story or ad in a few sentences [oai_citation:24‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=When%20you%27re%20ready%20and%20happy,Synopsis%20to%20see%20the%20updates). This synopsis encapsulates the core idea: for an ad, it might summarize the scenario and message; for a narrative, it outlines the premise. **Review and edit** this synopsis as needed – you can directly edit the text to add details or adjust the focus [oai_citation:25‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=When%20you%27re%20ready%20and%20happy,Synopsis%20to%20see%20the%20updates). Any changes you make will carry forward into subsequent steps (they influence the next stage of generation). If you edit the concept or synopsis heavily, you may regenerate the synopsis to see an updated version reflecting your changes [oai_citation:26‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=will%20create%20a%20concise%20summary,Synopsis%20to%20see%20the%20updates).

- **Scene Breakdown (Beats/Scenes Tab):** Satisfied with the synopsis, you proceed to generate a **scene-by-scene breakdown** by clicking **“Generate Scenes.”** The AI uses the synopsis (and your earlier inputs) to **outline the narrative into discrete scenes or beats** [oai_citation:27‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Once%20satisfied%2C%20click%20) [oai_citation:28‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Your%20story%20is%20taking%20shape%21,Scene%2C%2060%201%20min%20episodes). In a short film, this might come out as a sequence of scenes (e.g. Scene 1: Setup, Scene 2: Conflict, Scene 3: Climax/Resolution if following a three-act structure). In an ad, it might break the 30-second spot into a few key moments or beats (Problem → Solution → Call to Action, for example). The **Story Type and structure you selected earlier influences this** – e.g. if you chose a 3-act structure, the scenes might be organized into Act I, II, III; if it’s an Ad, it might be a single-act with a few shots, etc. [oai_citation:29‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Your%20story%20is%20taking%20shape%21,Scene%2C%2060%201%20min%20episodes) [oai_citation:30‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=by,Scene%2C%2060%201%20min%20episodes). 

  The scene breakdown will list each scene title or beat with a short description of what happens or what point it conveys. You should **review this outline and feel free to edit it** – you can rename scenes, add or remove scenes, or tweak descriptions to better fit your vision [oai_citation:31‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=structure%20,60%201%20min%20episodes) [oai_citation:32‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Review%20%26%20Edit%20Scene,scene%2C%20or%20delete%20a%20scene). This step is your high-level storyboard in text form. For example, for the lemonade ad, the breakdown might be:
  - *Scene 1: The Problem* – Aria struggling to sell lemonade.
  - *Scene 2: The Spark* – She gets an idea (superhero theme) to promote it.
  - *Scene 3: The Success* – The ad showcases the successful lemonade stand with a fun twist.
  
  You can edit or rearrange these as needed. Once the scene-by-scene outline looks good, move on to script generation.

- **Full Script Writing (Script Tab):** Now click **“Generate Full Script.”** Using the detailed scene outline, the AI Co-Pilot expands each scene into a **full script**, including scene descriptions, action lines, dialogues, and even camera directions or transitions if appropriate [oai_citation:33‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,button%20to%20expand%20the%20script). Essentially, the platform writes a screenplay-style script for you. **Review and refine the script** in the built-in script editor: you can edit any dialogue lines, tweak the narration, or shorten/lengthen scenes. The editor supports a fullscreen mode for easier writing [oai_citation:34‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=Head%20to%20the%20Script%20tab,dialogue%2C%20action%20lines%2C%20and%20transitions). For ads, the advice is to keep scripts **short and snappy** to fit the desired runtime [oai_citation:35‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=including%20dialogue%2C%20action%20lines%2C%20and,transitions), whereas for narratives you may flesh out more detail. All changes are auto-saved. At this stage, ensure character names, lines, and story flow are as you want, because the next steps will use this script to generate visuals.

Throughout concept development, you maintain **creative control**: you can iterate back and forth – e.g. go back to the Concept tab to add a detail and regenerate synopsis/scenes, or adjust the outline and regenerate the script. MovieFlo allows going back to previous steps and regenerating subsequent outputs to reflect changes (though keep in mind you’ll then need to regenerate things like actors or storyboards that were based on the old script) [oai_citation:36‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Iterative%20Process%3A%20You%20can%20always,images%20with%20the%20old%20actors) [oai_citation:37‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,try%20editing%20the%20Prompt%20directly). By the end of this phase, you have a completed **script and scene breakdown**, which serves as the blueprint for visual production.

## Creative Controls – Structuring Story, Genre, Tone, Style, and Aspect Ratio

MovieFlo.AI gives creators a lot of **control over the creative direction** at both the project level and throughout the workflow. These “creative controls” ensure the output matches your vision in terms of narrative structure and stylistic feel. Key creative options include:

- **Story Structure & Length:** As mentioned, you can choose between an AI-decided structure, a traditional Act-based structure, or episodic. For example, selecting a **3-Act structure** will cause the AI to generate three distinct act sections in the story breakdown [oai_citation:38‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=individual%20shot%20via%20a%20pulldown,menu). The **duration** setting also guides how the script is paced – e.g. a 15-second social media ad vs. a 2-minute short film will have different pacing and number of scenes. You can adjust these parameters in project settings if you decide to change the format (though altering duration late may require trimming or expanding script content manually).

- **Genre:** Defining a genre focuses the content into a particular storytelling tradition or category [oai_citation:39‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=8.%20Genre%20and%20Tone%20,emotional%20impact%20of%20your%20script). For instance, if you set Genre to “Horror,” the script and visuals will incorporate horror elements (e.g. suspenseful moments, darker imagery), whereas “Comedy” will yield a lighter tone and likely comedic dialogue. MovieFlo supports a wide range of genres – drama, action, romance, fantasy, sci-fi, mystery, etc. – and you can input custom genres too. This helps the AI tailor the narrative style (plot devices, character archetypes) accordingly.

- **Tone:** The tone slider/field lets you indicate the mood or emotional tone of the piece [oai_citation:40‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=8.%20Genre%20and%20Tone%20,emotional%20impact%20of%20your%20script). Examples of tone descriptors: *uplifting, somber, humorous, epic, scary, educational,* etc. Tone influences how scenes are written (e.g. dialogue and narration might become more serious or more playful) and how scenes are visualized (color schemes, lighting, and music suggestions might change). You have full control to combine genre and tone (e.g. *Genre: “Superhero”, Tone: “Inspirational”* could produce a heroic, motivational style).

- **Visual Style (Art Style):** This is a powerful control over the **aesthetic look** of all images and videos. MovieFlo offers numerous visual style presets, from realistic cinematic looks to stylized or animated looks. For example, you can choose **Photorealistic** (for live-action vibe), or styles like **Film Noir**, **16mm Vintage**, **1980s TV**, **Cyberpunk**, **Documentary** realism, or go artistic with **Anime (shōnen or moe)**, **Pixar-like 3D animation**, **Claymation**, **Comic Book**, **Voxel art**, etc. [oai_citation:41‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,of%20styles%20can%20I%20create) [oai_citation:42‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=9.%20Style%20,for%20each%20Story%20you%20create). The chosen style will be reflected in the AI-generated storyboards and character designs. This helps maintain a consistent art direction – if you pick *Claymation*, all characters and scenes will be rendered to look like clay stop-motion, whereas *Cyberpunk Neon-Noir* will give you high-contrast futuristic city visuals, and so on. You can always experiment by changing the style later (in the Settings) and regenerating images to compare results [oai_citation:43‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,for%20this%20to%20take%20effect) [oai_citation:44‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,before%20you%20made%20the%20change). This feature essentially replaces the need for separate concept artists or style guides – the AI will enforce the style across all assets automatically.

- **Aspect Ratio & Output Format:** The **aspect ratio** is crucial for framing. In MovieFlo’s project settings, you select the aspect ratio (e.g. 16:9 widescreen vs 9:16 vertical) at start, or it’s auto-set by choosing an output target like TikTok (vertical) or YouTube (widescreen) [oai_citation:45‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=4.%20Output%20Target%20,YouTube%2C%20instagram%2C%20Tiktok%20and%20more). The aspect ratio will constrain how images and videos are generated (for example, vertical framing will compose shots differently than horizontal). If you need to change aspect ratio mid-project, you can, but you’ll have to clear or regenerate all storyboard images and videos to the new format [oai_citation:46‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=have%20to%20regenerate%20any%20image,for%20this%20to%20take%20effect). The platform uses the aspect ratio to ensure that when you export, the video is already in the correct dimensions for your target medium. Additionally, output targets might set resolution limits appropriate for those platforms.

- **Models and Technical Settings:** While not a creative “story” element, the ability to choose default **AI models** (for image, video, voice) is another form of creative control. Some models are better at certain styles or content (e.g. a cartoonish video model vs. a photorealistic one). In MovieFlo, you can switch models per shot (more on this later) to fine-tune the output style or quality [oai_citation:47‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=You%20can%20select%20different%20models,project%2C%20giving%20you%20full%20flexibility). This is a unique control – it means if one scene needs a hyper-real look and another an illustrative look, you are free to use different generative models to achieve that, all within one project.

All these creative controls work in tandem. For instance, you might set **Genre: “Adventure”**, **Tone: “Light-hearted”**, **Style: “Pixar 3D”**, **Structure: 3-Act**, **Aspect: 16:9**. The AI will then generate a script in three acts with adventurous, family-friendly storytelling, and when you generate visuals, it will use a 3D animated style. If at any point the output isn’t aligning with your vision, you can tweak these parameters and regenerate content. MovieFlo’s strength is giving **high-level creative direction** to the AI, so you get the kind of story and look you want *without* having to micromanage every line or pixel – while still being able to dive in and edit anything manually.

## Asset Creation – Actors, Characters, and Visual References

With a script in hand, the next major phase is **creating the visual assets**, particularly the **characters (actors)** and any important props or reference images needed for consistency. MovieFlo.AI excels at maintaining character consistency: it generates “actors” that will persist in every scene and shot, so the same character looks the same throughout the video [oai_citation:48‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/filmmakers-storytellers#:~:text=Custom%2C%20consistent%20Actors) [oai_citation:49‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Guaranteed%20Consistency). Here’s how the asset creation works:

 *Screenshot: The **Actors** tab for a sample project (“Aria’s Legendary Lemonade”) showing multiple character images and a logo. MovieFlo automatically extracted the main characters from the script (e.g. Aria, Dad, etc.) and generated concept art for each. The interface allows adding new actors or uploading images (e.g. the “Aria’s Lemonade” logo is treated as an actor for consistency) [oai_citation:50‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=,images%2C%20or%20props%20here%20too) [oai_citation:51‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=Image%3A%20Screenshot%20of%20a%20character,creating%20or%20editing%20character%20images).*

- **Automatic Character Identification:** When you move to the **Actors** tab, MovieFlo’s AI parses your script and **auto-identifies the key characters** (and even notable proper nouns or entities) in the story [oai_citation:52‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=,identified%20based%20on%20the%20script). These become your project’s list of “Actors.” For each such character, the system will create an **Actor Profile** consisting of a name, a textual description, and image slots. In our example, it detected “Aria” (the 8-year-old protagonist), her “Dad”, a group of “Dancers” mentioned in the script, a “Pirate Captain” (perhaps a metaphor or fantasy element in the story), and even the product/logo “Aria’s Lemonade” itself. All of these appear as actor cards in the UI (as shown above), ready to be fleshed out.

- **Generate from Story (AI Character Images):** For each actor, you can have the AI generate concept art by clicking **“Generate from Story.”** The Co-Pilot Flo will use the character’s description (derived from context in your script) plus the overall style you chose to formulate an image prompt [oai_citation:53‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,options%20to%20create%20their%20look) [oai_citation:54‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,AI%27s%20suggestion%2C%20then%20tweak%20it). With one click, it produces a character image. For example, for Aria it might generate an image of a little girl matching the script’s details (blonde hair, energetic, wearing a cape if the story suggested that). This is effectively AI casting – creating the look of each character. You can regenerate alternatives if you don’t like the first result, or edit the prompt yourself for more control [oai_citation:55‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,AI%27s%20suggestion%2C%20then%20tweak%20it) [oai_citation:56‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,to%20the%20prompt%20as%20well). The interface even keeps a **Generation History** for each actor, so you can review all the past images you generated for that character along with the prompts used [oai_citation:57‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=) [oai_citation:58‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,used%20to%20make%20that%20generation). This helps in comparing versions or rolling back to a previous look if needed.

- **Create from Photo (Using Reference Images):** Alternatively (or additionally), you can upload a **reference photo** to base a character’s appearance on. Clicking **“Create from Photo”** lets you drag-and-drop an image file as a reference for that actor [oai_citation:59‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,clearly%20distinct%20from%20the%20background). This could be a photo of a real person you want the character to resemble (ensuring you have rights/permission), or a drawing/concept art you have. Once you upload, you hit “Suggest” and the AI will generate an image prompt that tries to **match the photo’s subject** to your chosen art style [oai_citation:60‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,clearly%20distinct%20from%20the%20background) [oai_citation:61‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Image%3A%20Young%20woman%20with%20long,standing%20against%20a%20neutral%20background). You can even choose to **keep the photo’s original outfit** or use the character’s costume from the story [oai_citation:62‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,in%20the%20photo%20is%20wearing). For example, if Aria was based on a real child’s photo, the system could generate an image of that child in the animated style of your project. This feature is useful for including brand mascots, real actors’ likenesses (for internal testing), or product images in your story.

- **Editing Actor Details:** For each actor, you have a text description (bio) that the system extracted from the script. You can click the actor’s name or info to **edit their description – including appearance, personality, and role** [oai_citation:63‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,appearance%2C%20role%20in%20the%20story) [oai_citation:64‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,appearance%2C%20role%20in%20the%20story). Changing this description can alter the prompt used for image generation (if you click “Suggest” again) [oai_citation:65‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Suggest%20button%20below%20the%20prompt) [oai_citation:66‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=appearance%2C%20but%20also%20about%20personality%2C,Suggest%20button%20below%20the%20prompt). For instance, you might add “wears a yellow superhero cape with a smiley-face logo” to Aria’s description to ensure the images include that detail. Fine-tuning the text description is a powerful way to guide the AI’s image generation for consistency and specificity. After editing, you can regenerate the actor’s image to reflect the changes.

- **Multiple Images & Variants:** You’re not limited to one image per actor. You can generate multiple variants and scroll through the **actor’s image gallery** (the UI will show all images made for that actor). There’s also an **image editing tool**: by clicking a pencil icon on a generated actor image, you can do a **“variation” generation** – for example, keep the same actor but *change one aspect* like hair color, clothing, or expression [oai_citation:67‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Actor%E2%80%99s%20hair%20color%20or%20clothing). The system will prompt you for what change you want and then regenerate a new image with that tweak. This iterative approach helps nail down the exact look you want for the character.

- **Assigning Voices:** In the Actors tab, you can also assign a **voice** to each character upfront. MovieFlo provides a library of **140+ AI voice options** (covering different genders, ages, and accents) [oai_citation:68‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/filmmakers-storytellers#:~:text=Voice%2C%20music%2C%20and%20lip%20sync,included). You can click the Voice dropdown for an actor to preview voices and select one that fits (for example, choose a youthful, energetic voice for Aria) [oai_citation:69‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,in%20the%20Lip%20Sync%20section). This will be used later if you generate text-to-speech lines for that character. (If you prefer, you can also leave it unassigned and later upload a real voice recording or choose at the lip-sync stage; assigning here just streamlines the process). The ability to set voices per character means each character’s dialogue will be spoken in a consistent voice during video generation.

- **Adding Additional Assets:** The Actors section isn’t limited to people. **You can add any important asset as an “actor”** to leverage the consistency engine [oai_citation:70‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=,images%2C%20or%20props%20here%20too) [oai_citation:71‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=editing%20character%20images). For instance, in the lemonade ad example, the *logo* “Aria’s Lemonade” was added as an actor – uploading the logo image so it can appear on signage consistently. You could do the same for a key prop (like a unique car, a fictional creature, or a specific location’s reference photo). Currently, Flo auto-detects only characters from the script, but the team plans to have it auto-detect key props and locations in the future for you [oai_citation:72‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,now%20by%20adding%20them%20manually). In the meantime, you can manually use the **“Create Actor”** button to add any item. By doing so, you ensure that when you generate scenes, the AI knows what that item looks like and will include it correctly (for example, always using the same logo design on a bottle in every shot).

After creating or uploading all your actors and key assets, you will have a **“casting folder”** of visuals. You should see all main characters each with at least one image that you’re happy with, and any references (logos, etc.) in place. This library of actors is crucial – it means when the AI creates storyboards and videos, it will **reuse these exact characters** rather than generating random people every time. This solves the usual problem with generative video of characters changing appearance; MovieFlo locks the appearance via these actor images [oai_citation:73‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/filmmakers-storytellers#:~:text=Custom%2C%20consistent%20Actors). If at any point you want to try a different design for a character, you can come back to Actors, tweak or regenerate images, and those changes (after regenerating visuals) will flow through the whole film. **Once you are satisfied with your cast of characters, click “Generate Storyboards” to proceed** [oai_citation:74‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Ok%20once%20you%20are%20happy,Generate%29%20Storyboards).

## Scene and Shot Design – Storyboards and Shot Editing

Now begins the transformation of the written script into visual form. The **Storyboard** tab is where MovieFlo.AI breaks each scene into individual shots and lets you generate or edit each shot’s imagery. Effectively, this is your pre-visualization stage, equivalent to a storyboard artist drawing frames – except here the AI generates the storyboard frames for you. The workflow in this stage is as follows:

 *Screenshot: The **Storyboard** interface for the project. Scenes (or “Beats”) are listed with their shots. For example, “Beat 1: The Lemonpocalypse” has Shot 1 (Aria at her lemonade stand) and Shot 2 (Dad at his computer), etc. Each shot has a description and either a generated image or a placeholder. The top bar offers options to generate all descriptions or images in bulk, but typically users will edit shots one by one for control [oai_citation:75‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,generated) [oai_citation:76‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Select%20Shot%20,See%20below%20for%20more%20info).*

- **Auto-Generated Scene & Shot Descriptions:** When you first enter Storyboard mode, MovieFlo automatically uses your script to generate **shot descriptions for each scene**. It reads each scene in the script and figures out a reasonable sequence of shots to cover the action. These shot descriptions (a sentence or two each) describe what the camera would see in that shot [oai_citation:77‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,generated). For example, a scene described as “Aria sets up her lemonade stand on a sunny day” might be broken into Shot 1: Aria at the stand from the front, Shot 2: close-up of Dad thinking of an idea at his office (if the script cuts to dad), etc., as shown in the screenshot. The interface typically labels scenes as “Beat 1, Beat 2…” (or Act 1, Act 2 if applicable) with each containing one or more shots.

- **Bulk Generation Options (Use with Caution):** At the top of the Storyboard tab, there are quick gen buttons like **“Full Story Descriptions”**, **“Full Story Images”**, **“Scene Descriptions”**, **“Scene Images”** [oai_citation:78‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,eat%20up%20more%20credits%20rapidly). These allow one-click generation of all shot descriptions or images for a scene or even the entire story. However, the documentation warns to use these carefully because generating a lot of images at once will **consume a large number of credits quickly** [oai_citation:79‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,eat%20up%20more%20credits%20rapidly). It’s often better (especially on limited credits) to go shot-by-shot. Initially, since descriptions are already pre-filled, you may not need to press “Scene Descriptions” unless you’ve edited a scene and want the shots re-split. The typical flow is to **generate images one shot at a time for maximum control and cost efficiency** [oai_citation:80‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Step%201%3A%20Generate%20Storyboard%20Images).

- **Shot-by-Shot Editing:** You can click on any **individual shot** to open the **Edit Shot view**, which provides detailed controls for that shot [oai_citation:81‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Select%20Shot%20,See%20below%20for%20more%20info). In Edit view, you will see:
  - The **textual description of the shot** (what we call the shot prompt or storyboard text). This was generated from the script. *Note:* This description is **for your reference only – it doesn’t directly affect the image generation prompt** [oai_citation:82‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,always%20edit%20or%20change%20these). It’s basically a narrative explanation of the shot. You can edit it for clarity or storytelling, but it won’t change the image unless you also change the actual prompt (more on prompts below).
  - The list of **Actors tagged in the shot**. Flo will have automatically tagged which actors should appear in this shot based on the script (e.g. if the script says “Aria talks to her Dad,” both “Aria” and “Dad” will be pre-selected for that shot) [oai_citation:83‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). You should verify these: ensure all characters who should be visible in that shot are checked, and remove any that shouldn’t. Proper actor tagging is important because only tagged actors’ images will be pulled into the prompt to appear. You can also **add an actor** if you think one should be present (for example, if you added a “Lemonade Stand” prop actor, you’d tag it in any shot of the stand).
  - Any **Dialogue** assigned to that shot. If the script had a line of dialogue for that shot, it will be shown (with the character name). This is mainly for your awareness and for lip-sync later, but you can also adjust the timing by moving dialogue to a different shot or splitting it. Dialogue text itself doesn’t directly change the image, except that the presence of a speaking character might influence their facial expression in video if lip-sync is applied. Here you can also add dialogue if you want a voiceover or line in this shot – it will carry into the lip-sync stage [oai_citation:84‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,It%E2%80%99s%20purely%20for%20storytelling%20purposes).
  - The **Image Generation Prompt** and settings. This is perhaps the most crucial part: the AI **prompt that will be used to generate the storyboard image**. When you first open a shot, Flo creates an initial prompt based on the scene description, the actors, and the global style. By default, the **image model** used is the one set as default (often *Kontext Pro*, which is good for concept art) [oai_citation:85‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=alter%20this%20dialogue%2C%20as%20desired,or%20add%20dialogue%20to%20shots). You can change the image model via a dropdown – a variety of latest image models are available (e.g. a faster but lower-res model or a very high-quality model). Note that each model has its own **credit cost per generation**, which is displayed next to the “Generate Shot” button for transparency [oai_citation:86‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=alter%20this%20dialogue%2C%20as%20desired,or%20add%20dialogue%20to%20shots). If you switch models, the prompt may auto-update to optimize for that model’s format (this auto-prompt-tuning can be turned off for advanced users) [oai_citation:87‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=comfortable%2C%20make%20edits%20to%20the,prompt%20as%20well) [oai_citation:88‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,out%20of%20a%20given%20model).
  - **Shot Details / Settings:** Depending on the model and the context, you may see extra settings like **camera angle, shot size, lighting, mood** etc. For example, there may be fields for “Shot Type” (wide shot, close-up, POV, etc.) or checkboxes for specific stylistic tweaks. These come from the script and default suggestions. Changing these will not instantly change the prompt; you should click **“Suggest”** (or an equivalent update button) to have the AI rewrite the prompt with the new details included [oai_citation:89‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=the%20image%20model%20being%20used) [oai_citation:90‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Shot%20Details%20,prompt%20with%20the%20new%20settings). This modular approach lets you adjust technical shot attributes without manually editing the raw prompt, if you prefer.

- **Generate Storyboard Image:** After reviewing/editing the above, you click **“Generate Shot”** to create the storyboard image for that shot [oai_citation:91‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,prompt%20and%20apply%20these%20changes). The AI will produce an image (using your selected model) that appears in the shot slot. **Evaluate the image**: does it match what you envisioned for that moment? If not, you have options:
  - You can hit **Regenerate** (often just clicking the button again) to have the AI try a different variation with the *same prompt*. Each generation can come out a bit different, so sometimes a second try gives a better framing or clarity [oai_citation:92‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Generate%29%20Shot).
  - Or you can **edit the prompt** manually. The prompt text is fully editable – you can type in more specific details (“Aria is smiling brightly, camera low angle, focus on lemonade logo”) or remove unwanted elements. After editing, hit Generate again to see the updated result [oai_citation:93‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Generate%29%20Shot). This trial-and-error is part of the creative process. The guide encourages iterating until the storyboard image **aligns with your vision**, because these images will directly influence the video generation next [oai_citation:94‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,when%20it%20turns%20into%20video). Essentially, treat these images as key frames – you want them to be right.
  - If there’s a *minor detail* to change, consider using the **edit (pencil) tool** on the image. Clicking the pencil on a storyboard image lets you make a slight adjustment request (like “make the lemonade sign text bigger” or “change house color to red”) and the AI will regenerate the image with that change [oai_citation:95‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=with%20your%20vision%20when%20it,turns%20into%20video). This uses inpainting or prompt editing under the hood so you don’t have to rewrite the whole prompt.
  - You can also move shots around (reordering) or add a new shot if you realize something’s missing. There’s an “Add Shot” placeholder after each scene sequence where you can insert a new shot and describe it.

- **Progress Through All Shots:** Navigate through each shot of each scene, generating images one by one. You can use “Next”/“Previous” arrows in the edit view or go back to the storyboard overview and click the next shot [oai_citation:96‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,by%20clicking%20Back%20to%20Storyboard). As you generate each, the storyboard panel fills out with images. At any point you can **preview the storyboard sequence**: by hovering over a generated shot image, if a video is generated it will play (for now it’s just a still), and you can also click a “Preview Mode” which plays a slideshow of all shots in order (once videos are generated it can auto-play the animatics) [oai_citation:97‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=) [oai_citation:98‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Auto,play%20through%20your%20story%20automatically).

By the end of this stage, you will have a **complete storyboard**: every shot has a representative image. Think of this like a flipbook or an animatic without motion – you can see your entire film/ad in still frames. This is a good point to review continuity (do characters look right in each shot? Does the sequence of shots tell the story clearly?). You can always go back and tweak any storyboard image. The platform emphasizes an **iterative process** – it’s okay to regenerate or refine images multiple times [oai_citation:99‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Iterative%20Process%3A%20You%20can%20always,images%20with%20the%20old%20actors) [oai_citation:100‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,try%20editing%20the%20Prompt%20directly). The credit cost is shown so you can manage your budget as you iterate.

## AI Video Generation – Bringing Storyboard Shots to Life

With storyboard images set, MovieFlo can now turn them into **animated video clips** using cutting-edge AI video generation models. This step essentially adds motion (and additional detail) to each storyboard frame, producing the clips that will make up your final video. The process for video generation is managed per shot (so you choose how to animate each storyboard image individually):

- **Choose a Video Model per Shot:** In the storyboard Edit Shot view, once an image is generated, you’ll see options to generate a **video**. There is a dropdown for the **video model** (the default might be something like *Kling v2.1 Standard*, a general-purpose model) [oai_citation:101‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Generate%29%20Video%20button). Different video models have different strengths: some are high-fidelity but slower (and cost more credits), others are fast or stylized. For example, *Veo 3* is known for realistic cinematic quality, *Kling* for smooth motion in animated styles, *Seedance* for quick previews, *Minimax (Hailuo)* for anime-style movement, etc. [oai_citation:102‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,AI%20generate%20visuals%20and%20video) [oai_citation:103‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=,for%20flagship%20video%20content). You have full flexibility to **pick the optimal model for each shot** [oai_citation:104‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=You%20can%20select%20different%20models,project%2C%20giving%20you%20full%20flexibility). You might use a photoreal model for one shot and a cartoon model for another if your project is stylistically varied. The UI will display the **credit cost** for generating that shot with the selected model (cost depends on model complexity, output resolution, and clip length) [oai_citation:105‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Generate%29%20Video%20button). For instance, a 5-second 720p clip on a simple model might cost ~30 credits, whereas an 8-second 1080p clip on a state-of-the-art model could cost a few hundred credits [oai_citation:106‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/model-guide#:~:text=Vidu%20Q1%201080p%20quality%2C%20fluid,10s%2030%20%2F%2060) [oai_citation:107‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/model-guide#:~:text=Veo%202%20Rich%20visuals%2C%20accurate,stylized%20visuals%2C%20consistent%20motion%2C%20fast). Plan your model choices according to the importance of the shot and your credit budget. *(The Model Guide in resources lists each model’s capabilities, max resolution/duration, and credit cost for reference [oai_citation:108‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=Use%20our%20AI%20Model%20Guide,optimize%20your%20workflow%20and%20results) [oai_citation:109‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,credit%20costs%20and%20output%20resolution).)*

- **Adjust Video Settings (Optional):** Similar to images, there are **video-specific settings and “Shot Details”** you can tweak. These might include camera motion (e.g. static, pan, zoom, dolly), shot speed (slow-motion or normal), and any other model-specific parameters (like “physics” for some models, or style consistency toggles) [oai_citation:110‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Shot%20Details%20,prompt%20with%20the%20new%20settings). The shot details section will pre-load some defaults based on the storyboard (for instance, if the storyboard prompt implied a camera angle, it might carry that in). If you change any of these settings via dropdowns, remember to click **Suggest/Update** to regenerate the video prompt with those changes [oai_citation:111‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Shot%20Details%20,prompt%20with%20the%20new%20settings). Essentially, the video prompt is an expanded version of the image prompt, plus these motion directives. Advanced users can edit the video prompt manually too, but usually the system handles it.

- **Generate the Video Clip:** Click **“Generate Video”** for the shot when ready [oai_citation:112‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Generate%20Video%20,for%20the%20video%20to%20generate). The AI will take the current storyboard image as a starting point and generate a short video clip (of the duration specified for that shot, e.g. 5 seconds). This typically takes a bit longer than image generation (several seconds to perhaps a minute, depending on model and length). Once done, the static image in the shot panel will be replaced by a playable video thumbnail. **Preview the clip** to check the motion and continuity. You can simply mouse-over the thumbnail to see it play, or open Preview Mode to watch it larger [oai_citation:113‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). In preview mode, you can play through all generated shots in sequence; there’s even an **auto-play** toggle to play the whole sequence continuously like a rough cut [oai_citation:114‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Preview%20Mode%20,shot%20to%20open%20Preview%20Mode) (with still frames for any shots you haven’t animated yet).

- **Review and Refine the Clip:** As with images, if the video result isn’t what you want, you have a few options:
  - **Regenerate with same settings:** Maybe the motion was a bit off or the subject looked glitchy – simply try generating again. The AI might produce a better take on the next attempt.
  - **Switch Models or Settings:** If a particular model isn’t handling the shot well (e.g. maybe the faces are inconsistent or the motion is choppy), you can try a different model. For example, if *Kling Standard* didn’t give sharp quality, you might try *Kling Pro* or *Veo 3 Fast*. Or if the shot feels too static, adjust a camera motion setting (e.g. add a slow zoom) and regenerate. This experimentation can dramatically change the outcome.
  - **Edit Video (Pencil Tool):** MovieFlo also provides a way to make slight changes to an existing video via the pencil icon (similar to image editing). For instance, you could request the video to be a bit slower or to focus more on a particular object. The platform will then generate a new variant video with that adjustment [oai_citation:115‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Editing%20Generated%20Videos%20,with%20a%20change%20to%20it). Keep in mind each video re-gen costs credits again.

- **Multi-Model Flexibility:** You can truly mix and match video models **shot by shot**. MovieFlo doesn’t lock you into one model for the whole project [oai_citation:116‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=You%20can%20select%20different%20models,project%2C%20giving%20you%20full%20flexibility). This is useful: e.g. use a **high-detail model for key shots** (like a close-up of the product in an ad or the climax scene in a film) and a **faster, lower-cost model for transitional shots or experimental angles**. The resulting clips will all come together in the editor, and because you maintained the same characters and style prompts, they should still feel coherent in sequence.

- **Generate All Shots:** Proceed through all your shots in all scenes, generating videos. You do not necessarily have to animate every single shot if, for example, you plan to use a static image somewhere intentionally. But generally for a finished video you’ll animate each storyboard panel. You’ll end up with a set of video clips – each just a few seconds long – corresponding to the whole story.

At this stage, you essentially have an **AI-generated rough cut** of your project: a series of clips, each typically 4–10 seconds, that together tell your story. You can watch the whole sequence in preview mode to see how it flows. The platform ensures **character consistency across these clips** by using the same actor images and prompts, so Aria looks like Aria in every shot and her outfit, setting, etc. remain consistent [oai_citation:117‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/filmmakers-storytellers#:~:text=Actor%20consistency%20engine) [oai_citation:118‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Guaranteed%20Consistency). Also, because you could adjust camera movements, you might have shots that pan or zoom giving it a more cinematic feel rather than a slide show. 

If something feels off continuity-wise (say, Aria’s position changes oddly between shot 1 and 2), you can go back – maybe add an intermediate shot, or adjust the prompt of one shot to better link to the next. This is akin to reshooting or adding coverage in traditional filmmaking. The ability to iterate quickly with AI models means you can **prototype multiple versions** of scenes. For instance, you could try one scene as live-action style and then re-generate the same scene in an anime style to compare, all within the same project (just save or duplicate the project first if you don’t want to lose the original). 

By the end of video generation, you have all the pieces of your video. Next comes adding sound and assembling the final cut.

## Editing and Assembly – Timeline Editor, Audio & Lip-Sync Integration

After generating the visual clips, MovieFlo provides a built-in **non-linear editor (NLE)** to arrange the clips, synchronize audio, and do final touches. This is the **Editor** tab, essentially a simplified timeline editing tool right in your browser. Here’s how you use it:

 *Screenshot: The **Editor** interface (timeline view). All storyboard shots (now videos) are laid out sequentially on the timeline. In this example, you see multiple clips of ~5 seconds each in order, with two empty audio tracks below. The preview window shows the current frame (Dad looking out window). You can trim clip lengths and add audio tracks (music, SFX, voice) by dragging files in [oai_citation:119‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,for%20you%20in%20the%20timeline) [oai_citation:120‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=).*

- **Automatic Timeline Assembly:** When you open the Editor, MovieFlo will have automatically placed all your generated **video clips (and any un-animated storyboard images)** onto the timeline in order [oai_citation:121‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,for%20you%20in%20the%20timeline). Essentially, it’s a rough cut assembled in the sequence of your storyboard. Each clip is represented as a strip on the timeline, labeled with the scene or shot. The default timing uses each clip’s full length as generated (e.g. if every shot was 5 seconds, you’ll see each as a 5s segment back-to-back). This gives you a starting point which you can play through in the preview monitor.

- **Trimming and Re-timing Clips:** You can **trim clips** to adjust pacing [oai_citation:122‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=already%20be%20sequentially%20laid%20out,for%20you%20in%20the%20timeline). Drag the edge of a clip on the timeline to shorten it (adjusting its out-point). For instance, maybe a generated clip came out 8 seconds but you only need 4 seconds of it for narrative timing – you can trim off the excess. (Trimming the start/in-point is also planned, but in the current beta editor you might only adjust the end; you could split a clip or regenerate shorter if needed). You can also **extend** image clips for longer duration (since a still frame can be held arbitrarily) [oai_citation:123‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,model%20used%20to%20generate%20them). Video clips, however, have a maximum length (the length they were generated with) – you cannot extend a 5s video to 10s unless you regenerate a longer clip, but you can slow down playback a bit if needed or loop it. The editor ensures you don’t accidentally extend beyond the generated content. By trimming, you tighten the rhythm of the story: remove any awkward pause or redundant movement and make the cuts snappier or slower as per your creative intent.

- **Reordering and Transitions:** Although the initial order follows the storyboard, you can rearrange clips on the timeline (drag and drop) if you want to change the sequence or omit a shot. The Editor is “non-linear” so you aren’t locked to the storyboard order. You can also insert black slugs or title cards by importing images if needed. Basic transitions like crossfades aren’t explicitly mentioned in documentation, but you can overlap two clips on different tracks to dissolve if the editor allows stacking; otherwise cuts are the primary transition.

- **Audio Integration – Voice and Soundtracks:** The Editor has multiple **audio tracks** below the video tracks【27†image】. This is where you add voiceovers, dialog clips, background music, and sound effects:
  - **Dialogue and Lip-Sync:** If you generated character dialogue with the text-to-speech feature, those voice lines will appear synchronized under the corresponding video clip automatically (each such clip with lip-sync carries its audio) [oai_citation:124‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,actor%20image%20will%20be%20highlighted) [oai_citation:125‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). For example, if Aria speaks in shot 1 and you did the lip-sync (more on that soon), an audio waveform for her voice is attached to that shot on the timeline. You should hear it when playing back. You can adjust its timing slightly if needed to better align mouth movements (though the lip-sync generation usually aligns it already).
  - **Background Music / SFX:** You can import external audio files (WAV or MP3) by dragging them into the **Assets/Import Audio** area or using an import button [oai_citation:126‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). Once imported, they become tracks you can drag onto the timeline. For instance, you might add a music track underneath the whole video for an ad jingle or a score for a short film. You can also add sound effect files at specific points (e.g. a “ding” when a lightbulb appears, or ambient street noise under a scene). The editor allows multiple audio tracks so you can layer music and SFX and voices.
  - **Adjusting Audio Timing:** Just like video, you can drag audio clips to position them, trim their in/out points, or split them. For example, align a music track to start at scene 1, or cut it right when the climax happens for effect. You might lower volume on one track while another plays (though volume control might be basic – possibly handled by editing externally if precise mixing is needed).
  - As you play back in the editor, **preview the full mix** – ensure dialogue is audible over music, timing matches the visuals, etc. This preview capability is crucial to make sure the AI-generated content and your added audio form a cohesive experience [oai_citation:127‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=1,transitions%20to%20music%20and%20mood).

- **Lip-Syncing Dialogue:** A standout feature is MovieFlo’s automatic **Lip-Sync** for talking characters. If your script has dialogue, you can use the Lip Sync tool so that the character’s video is generated with the mouth movements matching the line. The workflow is:
  1. In the Storyboard shot editor (before coming to timeline), go to the **Lip Sync panel** for a shot that has dialogue [oai_citation:128‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=If%20your%20ad%20has%20dialogue,sync%20it%20with%20your%20visuals). You will see the line of dialogue and the actor it’s assigned to. Ensure the correct **Actor is selected** (the one speaking) [oai_citation:129‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,before%20you%20add%20lip%20sync) and that this actor has a voice chosen (either you picked an AI voice for them earlier, or you plan to upload an audio file).
  2. If you want to use an AI voice: click **“Generate Voice Line.”** The system will take the text of the dialogue and synthesize it in the selected voice, producing an audio clip of that character speaking the line [oai_citation:130‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,files%20of%20your%20voiceover%20lines). You can play it to check if it sounds good. If the voice isn’t right, you can try a different voice selection and regenerate.
  3. Then click **“Generate Lip Sync.”** The AI will regenerate the video clip, this time animating the character’s face (mouth, and possibly subtle head movements) to match the spoken audio [oai_citation:131‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,button%20to%20test%20it%20out). This essentially produces a new video where the character delivers the line in sync. After it’s done, play back the video – you should see and hear the synchronized dialogue.
  4. If you have **your own recorded dialogue** (say you have a voice actor or yourself recording the lines), you can upload those instead of using the AI voice. There is an **option to upload .wav or .mp3 files** for each line [oai_citation:132‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,see%20below) [oai_citation:133‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,files%20of%20your%20voiceover%20lines). You’d then skip the “Generate Voice” step and go straight to generating lip-sync with the uploaded audio.
  
  Lip-sync can be done for any shot that has dialogue. The FAQ notes that currently the default lipsync model is Tavus (Hummingbird) with an alternative of Kling’s model [oai_citation:134‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Lip%20Sync), and more may be added. The important part is you must have generated a video first before lip-sync (lip-sync modifies an existing video) [oai_citation:135‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,other%20options%20in%20upcoming%20releases). Also, ensure each line is assigned to the right actor, especially if you have multiple characters speaking in one shot – you may need to split it into separate shots or generate combined lip sync accordingly.

- **Voiceovers/Narration:** If your project has a narrator or voiceover that isn’t a character in the scene, MovieFlo allows that too. The docs suggest a trick: create a new “Actor” named Narrator (or similar) purely for voiceover [oai_citation:136‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Adding%20a%20voiceover%20to%20any,in%20a%20few%20easy%20steps). Don’t generate images for this actor. In a shot where you want a voiceover, **assign this Narrator actor to the shot** (even though they have no on-screen presence) [oai_citation:137‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=2,want%20to%20have%20a%20voiceover), then add the narration text as “Dialogue” for the Narrator. Then, in Lip Sync panel, switch the mode from lip-sync to **Voiceover** for that shot [oai_citation:138‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=5,down%20menu%20and%20select%20Voiceover). When you generate the voice line and apply it, it will mix the voice into that shot’s video without trying to animate a face (since it knows it’s an off-screen voiceover) [oai_citation:139‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=5,down%20menu%20and%20select%20Voiceover). The result: you’ll have a narration audio over the visuals for that shot. This is perfect for things like a commercial voiceover or a documentary narrator. Each such shot with voiceover will produce a new video file with the audio embedded, which then shows up on the timeline with the voice on an audio track. You can use the Editor to adjust volumes or spans of these as needed.

- **Previewing the Edited Video:** The editor allows you to **play back the entire sequence** with all the cuts and audio in place [oai_citation:140‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=1,transitions%20to%20music%20and%20mood). Use this to fine-tune the timing: ensure that scene transitions feel right, music hits at the correct moments, and lip-synced lines align. You might find you need to trim a bit more here or there, or maybe extend a clip slightly to match a music beat (note: you cannot extend a video beyond its generated length, but you can pause on its last frame by converting it to an image if absolutely necessary, or regenerate a longer version if you have credits). The preview is essential to catch any issues before final export.

The built-in editor is a newer feature and somewhat basic compared to pro software – for instance, it might not have advanced color grading or text overlay tools yet (it’s primarily for arranging the AI-generated content) [oai_citation:141‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=This%20is%20where%20you%20bring,Premiere%2C%20FinalCu%20t%2C%20CapCut%2C%20Etc). The MovieFlo team suggests that for very fine editing or further polish, creators can export the project and finish in an external app like Adobe Premiere or Final Cut [oai_citation:142‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Premiere%2C%20FinalCu%20t%2C%20CapCut%2C%20Etc). However, the inclusion of the editor means you can get to a **complete, watchable video entirely within MovieFlo** without needing other software, which is especially convenient for quick projects or prototyping. It also supports a collaborative workflow – the FAQ mentions you can share projects or even transfer project ownership to a teammate to let them continue editing [oai_citation:143‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,collaborative) [oai_citation:144‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,Image%20and%20Video%20generators%20here), and team members can leave **shot-specific notes** on the storyboard (there’s a Shot Notes field) for feedback or reminders [oai_citation:145‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=button%20above%20to%20re,prompt%20with%20the%20new%20settings).

By the end of the Editing & Assembly stage, you should have your video’s **picture lock and audio lock**, meaning the sequence and timing are final and all audio is in place. The only step left is to export the results for use outside the platform.

## Export Options – Final Output and Publishing

Once you’re happy with the edited video, MovieFlo.AI offers several **export and sharing options**. These allow you to download your work in various forms or publish it for others to see. In the **Export** tab, you’ll find:

- **Full Video Export:** The most straightforward option – export the entire project as a single video file (e.g. an MP4). When you choose **“Full Movie” export**, MovieFlo will render all the scenes and edits into one continuous video file that you can download [oai_citation:146‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Exporting) [oai_citation:147‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Individual%20Files%20,like%20images%20and%20video%20clips). This is convenient for immediately uploading the video to a platform or sharing it. The resolution will be what your project was set to (720p or 1080p typically, depending on models used and aspect ratio). If your project is short, the render is quick; longer projects may take a bit more time to stitch.

- **Individual Asset Export:** If you want to do further editing in another software or repurpose assets, you can choose **“Individual Files” export** [oai_citation:148‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Choose%20Export%20Type%20,Individual%20Files%20and%20Full%20Movie). This provides a **.zip file** containing all the source assets of your project: all the generated images, video clips (for each shot), and likely any audio files (like generated voiceovers) that were part of the project. It may also include the script or metadata. This option is great if you want to manually re-edit everything in, say, Premiere Pro from scratch – you’ll have each shot as a separate file which you can load into a timeline. It also means you retain the raw material (for example, you might use a particular AI-generated image as a promo poster, or reuse a video clip elsewhere).

- **Project File Export (XML for Editors):** For a smoother handoff to professional editing software, MovieFlo offers an **XML export** (often an Adobe Premiere Pro compatible XML) [oai_citation:149‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Choose%20Export%20Type%20,Individual%20Files%20and%20Full%20Movie) [oai_citation:150‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=exporting%3A%20XML%20Editor%2C%20Individual%20Files,and%20Full%20Movie). This is essentially a project file describing the edit sequence. So you can export an XML, then import that into Premiere or another NLE – it will recreate the timeline with cuts and asset placements as you had in MovieFlo. You’ll need the individual assets as well (the XML references them), which the export likely provides or uses from the zip. This option saves time in re-assembling the edit in a more powerful tool for finishing touches (color correction, advanced effects, etc.). It’s a bridge between MovieFlo’s environment and industry-standard workflows.

- **Cover Image (Poster) Generation:** A unique feature on export is the creation of a **cover poster image** for your story. The moment you open the Export tab, MovieFlo **auto-generates a poster image** using the key characters and theme of your story [oai_citation:151‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Cover%20Creation). It picks some of your main actors and composes a kind of movie-poster or thumbnail. You have tools to customize this:
  - You can **select which actors** to feature (up to 3–5 actors, depending on the image model limits) [oai_citation:152‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,cover%20image%20if%20you%20like) [oai_citation:153‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,allow%20up%20to%203%20Actors). For example, you might choose your protagonist and the product mascot for an ad poster.
  - The AI provides an initial **image prompt for the cover** based on your story’s content and style [oai_citation:154‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,allow%20up%20to%203%20Actors). You can tweak this prompt if you want a different emphasis.
  - Click **“Generate Cover Image”** to produce a new poster. It will generate an image (often in portrait orientation) that could serve as a thumbnail or cover art [oai_citation:155‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,your%20story%2C%20actors%2C%20and%20style). If you don’t like the first result, regenerate or adjust actors/prompt and try again. The cover generation uses image models (not video models), so it’s relatively quick and inexpensive in credits.
  - You can save the cover you like – it’s a nice final asset (for ads, it could be a social media thumbnail; for a film, a poster to showcase).

- **Publishing to MovieFlo Gallery:** MovieFlo.AI has an online **community gallery** or at least the capability to share your project on the platform (especially during the open beta) [oai_citation:156‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Movie%20Flo) [oai_citation:157‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Publish%20,Publish%20it%20to%20our%20community). In the Export tab, there’s a **“Publish”** section for this. To publish:
  - You select one of your cover images as the final poster (or generate a new one as described).
  - Add a **Title, Description, and Tags** for your video [oai_citation:158‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=If%20you%20want%20to%20showcase,your%20work%20inside%20MovieFlo) [oai_citation:159‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). Tags help categorize (e.g. #comedy, #AI, #ad, #anime) so others can find your video in the gallery. Some tags might be suggested by Flo based on your project.
  - Then click **“Publish Now.”** The project (likely the video and the cover, along with your title/description) will be published to your profile or the public gallery for others to view [oai_citation:160‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/how-to-tutorial#:~:text=,Tab) [oai_citation:161‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%28e,suggested). In open beta, this might earn you some credit rewards or just bragging rights – the site encourages sharing content. (You can choose not to publish or keep it unlisted if you prefer; future features may include privacy settings for published content [oai_citation:162‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,project%20details%20one%20last%20look).)

- **Download Formats:** Videos are usually MP4 (commonly H.264 codec) which is widely compatible. Images are PNG or JPG. The XML is in standard format. Audio might be WAV/MP3. The exact formats aren’t explicitly stated in the docs we saw, but those are standard.

After exporting, you have your finished video ready to use. If it’s an advertisement, you can now deploy it to TikTok or YouTube, etc. If it’s a short film or animatic, you have a video file to share with your team or audience. The entire pipeline from concept to final export has happened within MovieFlo.AI’s ecosystem, but you always have the option to take the outputs out at any stage.

One more neat aspect: **everything you created in the project is yours to use**. According to the MovieFlo team, **you retain full rights to the outputs** and can use them commercially as long as you abide by their terms [oai_citation:163‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,AI%20outputs%20commercially). The platform even provides a **commercial license** on paid plans so you don’t have to worry about usage rights for the AI-generated content. They also emphasize that they do not use your project data to train their models without consent, and your content remains private to you (unless you publish) [oai_citation:164‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Our%20AI%20Promise%3A%20Your%20Content,Period) [oai_citation:165‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=We%E2%80%99re%20committed%20to%20empowering%20creators,will%20be%20reused%20or%20mined).

At this point, having covered the workflow, we should look at how the service handles pricing, credits, and plan limitations, since that affects how one uses the system at each step.

## Plans and Credits – Free vs Paid Features and Usage Limits

MovieFlo.AI operates on a **credits system** for AI generation. Every time you generate something (whether a script, an image, a video, or a voice line), it costs a certain number of credits. The platform provides a few ways to get credits and has subscription plans that include monthly credits. Here’s a breakdown:

- **Free Tier:** MovieFlo is in open beta and offers a **Free plan** for new users. The free tier costs $0 and gives you a one-time allotment of **100 credits** to experiment with [oai_citation:166‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=START%20FOR%20FREE). This allows you to try out the workflow (generate some images and short clips) without paying. However, the free credits are modest – enough for perhaps a handful of images and a couple of very short video generations. High-resolution or longer clips cost many credits (for example, one 8-second HD video on a high-end model could exceed 100 credits by itself [oai_citation:167‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/model-guide#:~:text=Vidu%20Q1%201080p%20quality%2C%20fluid,stylized%20visuals%2C%20consistent%20motion%2C%20fast)). So the free plan is mainly for testing. The Free plan outputs also come with **limitations**: they likely have a **watermark** on generated videos/images and do not come with commercial usage rights by default (since paid plans explicitly list “watermark removal” and “commercial license” as benefits [oai_citation:168‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=650%20Credits%20%2F%20month)). This means if you’re using the free tier, your videos will have a MovieFlo.AI logo or watermark, and you’d need to upgrade to use the content commercially or remove that branding.

- **Credit Packs:** If you run out of free credits but aren’t ready for a subscription, you can purchase one-time **credit top-up packs**. The site lists several packs, for example: **750 credits for $10**, **2000 credits for $25**, **4500 credits for $50**, up to **10,000 credits for $99**, etc. [oai_citation:169‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=,purchase). These packs let you pay as you go, and are handy if you just need a bit more to finish a project or if you want to scale up usage in bursts. The credits from packs just add to your balance. (In the UI, there’s an “Upgrade” or “Purchase Credits” button always showing your current credit balance and linking to purchase options [oai_citation:170‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Overview%20,top%20right%20of%20the%20app).)

- **Subscription Plans:** For heavier users, monthly subscriptions provide discounted credits and extra features:

This plan includes **watermark removal** and a **commercial license**, meaning anything you make can be used in ads, films, etc. without attribution and without a watermark [oai_citation:173‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=650%20Credits%20%2F%20month). It also grants **priority access to new features and models**, so Standard users might get to try new model updates earlier or have shorter wait times when servers are busy [oai_citation:174‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=,to%20New%20Features%20%2B%20Models). This plan is good for casual creators or small projects where ~650 credits (roughly ~80 images or a couple dozen short video generations) per month suffices.
  - **Pro Plan:** \$50/month (or \$40/month annual) for **2,500 credits/month** [oai_citation:175‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=Pro) [oai_citation:176‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=Pro). In addition to everything in Standard (no watermark, commercial use, priority access), Pro adds the ability to **clone projects** [oai_citation:177‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=,Cloning%20Projects) [oai_citation:178‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/pricing#:~:text=,Cloning%20Projects). ## Overall Framework – From Idea to Finished Video in One Platform

MovieFlo.AI’s workflow can be summarized as a **unified pipeline** that takes you **from concept to final video**, all within one web application [oai_citation:227‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Go%20from%20brief%20to%20script,hopping%20and%20focus%20on%20creating). It encapsulates the traditionally separate stages of film or video production – ideation, writing, storyboarding, casting, filming, editing, and post-production – into a streamlined digital process. Here’s a high-level look at how the platform organizes the production pipeline:

1. **Concept & Writing:** You begin with an idea or brief. MovieFlo provides an **AI Co-Pilot (Flo)** to brainstorm and refine this idea into a structured narrative. This replaces the need for separate creative writing tools or a writer’s room session by guiding you to a synopsis and script outline within the app [oai_citation:228‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=). You can start from scratch or bring a script – the system is flexible.

2. **Scripting & Story Structure:** The AI expands outlines into a full **screenplay-like script**, complete with scenes and dialogue. You have an integrated script editor to polish it. At the same time, the platform is aware of story structure (acts, beats) and ensures the script conforms to whatever format you set (commercial, short, episodic) [oai_citation:229‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=Your%20story%20is%20taking%20shape%21,Scene%2C%2060%201%20min%20episodes) [oai_citation:230‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=by,Scene%2C%2060%201%20min%20episodes). This means your story’s skeleton is built right inside MovieFlo, no need for Word docs or Final Draft software externally.

3. **Pre-visualization (Storyboards) with AI Images:** With a single click, the textual script is translated into visual plans – the **storyboard**. Instead of hiring a storyboard artist, you generate images for every shot using integrated **AI image models**. This happens in-app, shot by shot, with you in control of art style and content [oai_citation:231‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=). You can iterate quickly on these visuals, something traditionally very time-consuming. The storyboard stage in MovieFlo also doubles as a **shot planning tool** – you define camera angles, etc., in a structured way as you generate images.

4. **Casting & Asset Creation:** The platform ensures **consistency across frames** by introducing the concept of **Actors (Characters)** that persist [oai_citation:232‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Guaranteed%20Consistency). This framework is like having a casting department and a continuity supervisor built in. You design each character once (either by AI generation or using reference photos) and that design is then referenced in every shot generation. So the overall framework keeps characters, props, and even color schemes consistent. It is a **unified asset library** approach: all your needed visuals (characters, logos, etc.) are stored as project assets and reused, ensuring the final video doesn’t have the common AI problem of a character looking different in each scene.

5. **Multi-model AI Engine Integration:** Under the hood, MovieFlo integrates a **“best-of-class” collection of AI models** (for text, image, video, audio) and lets you choose between them as needed [oai_citation:233‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=MovieFlo,art%20technology) [oai_citation:234‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=,for%20flagship%20video%20content). The user doesn’t have to worry about separate accounts or tools for these models – the framework abstracts it so that at each stage (text generation, image gen, video gen, voice gen) you have a dropdown of options. This is significant because it means the workflow is **model-agnostic** and future-proof: as new, better models come out (say a new version of a video model), the platform can slot it in, and users can immediately apply it to their shots. The overall pipeline remains the same, just with potentially improved results over time. It also means creators don’t have to manually move data between, say, MidJourney for images and Runway for video – MovieFlo is the single hub where all these capabilities converge [oai_citation:235‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=With%20an%20intuitive%2C%20guided%20workflow%2C,create%20consistently%2C%20creatively%2C%20and%20intuitively).

6. **Animation & Cinematic Video Generation:** After storyboards, each frame is brought to life with motion – this is analogous to the filming/animation stage in traditional production. MovieFlo’s framework allows per-shot refinement with different models and settings, essentially giving a **director’s control** to the user over how each shot is animated (realistic vs stylized, static vs camera movement, etc.) [oai_citation:236‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/filmmakers-storytellers#:~:text=Shot) [oai_citation:237‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,ads%2C%20anime%2C%20or%20cinematic%20styles). The pipeline ensures that these clips still fit together because they were based on a coherent storyboard and script. The heavy lifting (actual rendering of footage) is handled by the AI models on MovieFlo’s servers, but from the user perspective it’s just another step in the unified interface (no exporting prompts to another program – you hit “Generate Video” and get results inside the same app).

7. **Editing & Post-Production:** MovieFlo includes an **integrated video editor** which finalizes the production pipeline [oai_citation:238‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=). This means once clips are generated, you don’t need a third-party video editing software for basic assembly – you can do cutting, timing adjustments, and audio syncing right in the app. It effectively closes the loop, turning what the AI generated into a coherent film with sound. The presence of features like lip-sync and voice generation means even **audio post-production** is folded in – dialogue is synced, and you can add music. This is remarkable: in a traditional pipeline, you’d go from a VFX tool or animation tool into an editing tool and then into a sound mixing tool; MovieFlo’s framework condenses all of that. It might not have all advanced features yet (like fancy transitions or deep color grading), but it covers the crucial parts to reach a viewable final cut. And by offering export to professional formats, it doesn’t trap you – you can always take the project out to polish further.

8. **One-Click Export & Multi-format Output:** After editing, the pipeline culminates in export options that cater to different professional needs – whether you want a single video or the project assets or an editing project file [oai_citation:239‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/tutorial-guide#:~:text=%2A%20Choose%20Export%20Type%20,Individual%20Files%20and%20Full%20Movie). Also generating a cover image and enabling publishing means the pipeline even covers **distribution prep** (making a thumbnail, and uploading to a platform’s gallery). In essence, from the spark of an idea to sharing the finished video, every step is addressed in sequence on MovieFlo.AI.

Throughout this pipeline, MovieFlo.AI’s architecture enforces two key principles: **consistency** and **control**. Consistency is achieved by the way characters and styles are carried through each stage (brief-to-script consistency via AI memory, script-to-storyboard consistency via using the same context, image-to-video consistency via using initial images as keyframes, etc.) [oai_citation:240‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=Guaranteed%20Consistency). Control is given to the creator at each juncture – you’re not just hitting a “make video” button; you are directing the AI in an intuitive way: choosing styles, picking models, editing prompts, trimming timelines. This is why they market it as a “creative pipeline, unified” and not just a generator [oai_citation:241‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/#:~:text=With%20an%20intuitive%2C%20guided%20workflow%2C,create%20consistently%2C%20creatively%2C%20and%20intuitively). It’s built for creative professionals who want quality and specificity, not just generic outputs [oai_citation:242‡moviefloai.com](https://www.moviefloai.com/faq#:~:text=%2A%20,from%20other%20AI%20video%20tools).
