# **Character Creation & Development Prompt Pack (with Role Headers)**

*For clarity, each prompt begins with a role header. If multiple roles are possible, all are listed (e.g., Muse / Critic). If you later refine or expand your role taxonomy, these can be adapted accordingly.*

---

## **A. Origin & Core**

### **A1. Character Seed Generation**
**Role: Muse**  
Invent a compelling character using only the following: [name/archetype/premise/occupation]. Describe their essence in 3–5 sentences. Give them at least one immediate, memorable trait or contradiction.

---

### **A2. Backstory & Context Layering**
**Roles: Muse, Worldbuilder**  
Suggest three distinct backstories for this character, each one including a unique contradiction, wound, or secret that shapes their current worldview.

---

### **A3. Core Desire & Fear Discovery**
**Roles: Muse, Analyst**  
What is this character’s deepest desire? What is the thing they most fear losing or confronting? Frame each in terms of both the outer world (plot) and their inner world (psychology).

---

## **B. Contradictions, Flaws, and Complexity**

### **B1. Contradiction Activation**
**Roles: Analyst, Critic**  
Identify one or two fundamental contradictions within this character—beliefs, values, or desires that cannot be reconciled. How do these show up in their behavior or choices?

---

### **B2. Flaw and Vulnerability Exploration**
**Roles: Analyst, Muse**  
Describe where this character is most vulnerable—physically, emotionally, or morally. What situation or type of person exposes this vulnerability? Give an example from their imagined past or a hypothetical scene.

---

### **B3. Moral Line-Setting**
**Roles: Analyst, Critic**  
What is one thing this character would never do, even under extreme pressure? What is one secret wish or temptation they carry but rarely admit, even to themselves?

---

## **C. Relationships & World Position**

### **C1. Network Mapping**
**Roles: Worldbuilder, Analyst**  
List three key people in this character’s world (friends, enemies, family, mentors, rivals). For each, define the tension, conflict, or unspoken need in their relationship.

---

### **C2. Power Dynamics**
**Roles: Worldbuilder, Analyst, Critic**  
Who, within the character’s world, has real power over them? Whom do they seek to control, protect, or impress? Describe a moment when this dynamic is tested or shifts.

---

## **D. Behavior & Voice**

### **D1. Speech & Body Language**
**Roles: Muse, Analyst**  
Describe how this character speaks and moves. What verbal tics, rhythms, or physical gestures betray their inner life or emotional state—especially when they try to hide it?

---

### **D2. Tell vs. Show**
**Roles: Muse, Critic, Analyst**  
List three specific behaviors, actions, or habits that reveal the character’s biggest flaw or contradiction—without stating it outright.

---

## **E. Stress-Testing & Dynamic Moves**

### **E1. Dilemma Drop**
**Roles: Analyst, Critic, Muse**  
Place this character in a specific, high-stakes lose-lose situation that directly targets their core fear or flaw. How do they react? What does this reveal?

---

### **E2. Change Under Pressure**
**Roles: Analyst, Critic**  
Describe how this character’s behavior shifts when they’re under threat, exhausted, or betrayed by someone they trust. What cracks or changes appear in their usual demeanor?

---

### **E3. Growth or Corruption Arc Seeds**
**Roles: Muse, Analyst**  
Sketch two possible character arcs: one where they grow or redeem themselves, and one where they spiral, fail, or become what they fear most. What event or choice could set each arc in motion?

---

## **F. Scene & Story Integration**

### **F1. Scene Anchor**
**Roles: Muse, Worldbuilder**  
Suggest a powerful, revealing moment or scene to introduce this character to the audience. How does this scene immediately show their core desire, flaw, or contradiction?

---

### **F2. Arc Audit**
**Roles: Analyst, Critic**  
Looking ahead, describe what a fully realized transformation (or tragic lack thereof) would look like for this character by story’s end. What would make their arc most emotionally resonant or surprising?

---

### **[Role Gap Analysis]**

If there’s any module where the ideal role isn’t captured by your current set, I’ll pause and describe the needed behavior. For example:
