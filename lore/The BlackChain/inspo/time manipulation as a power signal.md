Yes — those two patterns are very much in the same *family* of tactics as what the Roy kids do in that *Succession* scene, just scaled to geopolitical proportions.  

You’re pointing at two related but distinct mechanisms:  

---

**1. Making others physically wait**  
- *Putin:* Frequently keeps visiting leaders waiting — sometimes for hours — before a meeting.  
- This signals that **his time is more valuable than theirs**, and forces the other party to start the interaction slightly off balance and in a submissive frame.  
- Even if the wait is artificially created (he’s in the building but “not ready”), the psychological effect is real.  

**Succession parallel:** The Roy kids delay the investors not because they need to strategize, but to reframe the room so they enter as the prize, not the applicant.  

---

**2. Delayed verbal response**  
- *Putin:* Withholds comment on major events, sometimes for weeks.  
- This forces media, allies, and adversaries to fill the silence themselves, speculating about his stance.  
- When he finally speaks, he controls the framing — the “final word” feels more authoritative because it comes after the initial noise has died down.  

**Succession parallel:** While less drawn-out in time, the kids’ “hold on, we need to talk to our sister” is a *micro-delay in communication*, forcing the investors to sit in uncertainty and making the siblings’ eventual “pitch” seem more decisive and curated.  

---

Both hinge on **asymmetry of urgency**:  
> *I am in no rush, but you are — and that difference defines who holds power.*
